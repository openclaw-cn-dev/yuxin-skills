# Chat Scripts (聊天话术)

Short, friendly, one question at a time. Adapt placeholders; never invent facts.

## Principles

- Mirror their language and tone; keep messages to 1–3 short lines.
- Use their name once early, then sparingly.
- Ask before you pitch; recommend one option, not five.
- Answer the question asked first, then add one useful detail.
- Voice notes and long PDFs only if they ask.
- End most messages with a simple question or clear next step.

## B2C flow (fictional CoreSeat, $49)

**1. Opener (after their first message)**
> Hi Jess! Thanks for messaging 😊 Is this for your desk chair, your car, or both?

**2. Discovery**
> Got it. Is it more lower back or tailbone that bothers you after a long day?

**3. Recommendation**
> Then the CoreSeat is a good match: the U-shaped cutout takes pressure off the tailbone, and the foam doesn't flatten. Want the link with free shipping?

**4. Proof (if hesitant)**
> Totally fair to wonder. Here's a quick video from a customer who drives 3 hours a day: [link]. And you get 30 days to try it. If it doesn't help, send it back.

**5. Close**
> Here's your checkout link: [link]. It ships from our US warehouse in 1–2 days. Anything else I can help with?

**6. Follow-up (no reply after 24–48 h)**
> Hi Jess, just checking in. Any questions about sizing or shipping before you decide?

**7. Last follow-up (after 4–5 days)**
> No pressure at all! I'll leave it here. If your back starts complaining again, I'm one message away 🙂

**8. After purchase**
> Your order's on the way 🎉 Tip: set it so the cutout faces the back of the chair. Let me know how it feels after a few days!

**9. Review / referral (after 14 days)**
> Hi Jess! How's the cushion working out? If it's helping, a quick review would mean a lot to our small team: [link]

## B2B flow (fictional Brightwell Drinkware, MOQ 300)

**1. Inquiry reply**
> Hi Megan, thanks for reaching out! Happy to help. Which sizes are you considering, and roughly how many units for the first order?

**2. Qualify**
> Great. Do you need your logo on them, and when would you want them in store?

**3. Quote message**
> Here's a quick summary for 500 × 20 oz tumblers with a laser-engraved logo:
> • Unit price: $X (FOB) / $Y (delivered to your store)
> • Lead time: about 25 days production + shipping
> • Samples: 2 pieces, sample fee credited on your first order
> Want me to send the full quote as a PDF?

**4. Sample push**
> The easiest next step is samples, so you can feel the quality. Which two colors should I send?

**5. Follow-up after samples**
> Hi Megan, the samples should have arrived. What did you and the team think?

**6. Close**
> If you're ready, I'll send the proforma invoice and lock the production slot for {date}. Should I go ahead?

## Objection handling

| Objection | Reply |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive." | "Totally get it. Most cheaper cushions flatten in a few weeks; ours is made to hold up for months, and you have 30 days to try it. Want me to show you a side-by-side?" |
| "I'll think about it." | "Of course! Is there anything specific you're unsure about? Happy to answer now so you have everything you need." |
| "Is this legit? Never heard of you." | "Fair question! We're a small brand, here are recent reviews with photos [link], and you can pay with PayPal or your card for buyer protection." *(only list protections you actually offer)* |
| "Shipping takes too long." | "It ships from our US warehouse in 1–2 days and usually arrives in 3–5. Here's tracking info from a recent order if useful." |
| "Can you do a discount?" | "We keep prices the same for everyone, but the bundle with the lumbar pillow saves $19 if you'd use both." *(only if true; follow the business's discount policy)* |
| B2B: "Your price is higher than another supplier." | "Thanks for telling me. Can I ask what's included in their quote? Our price includes logo engraving, drop-testing and US warehouse reorders in 7 days, so it may not be like-for-like." |
| B2B: "MOQ is too high." | "I understand. For a first order we can do 300 with two colors split. Would that work for a test?" *(only if the business allows)* |

## Handling complaints

1. Acknowledge and apologize for the experience: "I'm really sorry about that."
2. Ask for what you need in one message (order number, photo).
3. Offer a clear fix and a timeline.
4. Confirm it's resolved, then thank them.
Move heated conversations to email or a call if needed; never argue in public comments.

## Things not to say

- "Dear Sir/Madam", "Kindly do the needful", "Why you not reply?"
- Pressure lines: "Only today!" (unless true), "Last chance!!!"
- Claims you can't back up: medical outcomes, "best quality in the world"
- Asking for payment outside normal, protected channels
