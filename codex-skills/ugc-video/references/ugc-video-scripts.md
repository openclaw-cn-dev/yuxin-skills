
# UGC Video Scripts

## Goal

Short-form video scripts that look like native content, plus a creator brief clear enough that a creator can deliver usable ads on the first try.

## Before you start: gather context

- Product, key benefit, proof, offer
- Target viewer and their main objection
- Platform and length (most ads: 15–45 seconds)
- Who will film: the user, a hired creator, or existing customers

## Script structure

| Part | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Stop the scroll: visual + spoken + on-screen text |
| Problem | 3–8 s | Make the viewer feel seen |
| Product moment | 8–20 s | Show it in use, close up, real hands |
| Proof | 20–30 s | Result, review, number, comparison |
| CTA | last 3–5 s | One clear next step, plus the offer |

## The 3-second conflict rule (non-negotiable)

The first 3 seconds must contain a dramatic conflict: tension between what the viewer expects and what happens, between two people, or between a person and a problem. A hook with no conflict does not stop the scroll and kills retention. Examples of conflict:

- Person vs problem: the product fails in the old way on screen ("third time this week my phone died at 20%")
- Person vs person: a partner, roommate or customer pushes back ("You spent HOW much on that?")
- Expectation vs reality: something looks wrong, then turns out right
- Stakes and a ticking clock: "I have 10 minutes before the guests arrive and the sink is clogged"

Check every hook: if you remove the conflict and nothing is lost, rewrite it. To rebuild a script from an existing video, use `references/ugc-video-teardown.md`.

## Hook types

- Bold statement: "I wasted $200 before I found this."
- Question tied to a problem (product-focused, not about the viewer's personal traits)
- Visual pattern interrupt: unexpected action in the first frame
- "POV:" scenario
- "Things I wish I knew before buying a…"
- Reacting to a comment or review
Write at least five hooks per script; the same body can be tested with each.

## Formats

Talking head, unboxing, "3 reasons", side-by-side comparison, day-in-the-life, green-screen reaction, tutorial, problem-solution skit. Avoid exaggerated before/after claims in health and beauty categories.

## Creator brief (template)

- **Product and the one thing to get across**
- **Talking points** (not a word-for-word script; let creators speak naturally)
- **Must show**: product close-up, product in use, packaging if relevant
- **Do not**: claims to avoid, competitor names, unlicensed music
- **Deliverables**: number of videos, length, raw footage, hook variations, 9:16 vertical, captions off for editing
- **Usage rights**: paid ads usage period, Spark Ads / partnership ads authorization, whitelisting
- **Disclosure**: creators must disclose paid partnerships according to FTC rules and platform tools
- **Deadline and payment**

## Rules of thumb

- Real people in real settings outperform studio polish on TikTok and Reels.
- Subtitles always; many people watch without sound.
- One message per video. If there are three benefits, make three videos.
- Never script fake testimonials; claims of results must reflect real customer experiences.

## Output format

1. 3–5 scripts, each as a table: time, visual / shot, spoken line, on-screen text
2. Five hook alternatives per script
3. A filled creator brief
