# Toxin Profiles Reference — red-tide-algal-bloom-monitoring

## Neurotoxic Shellfish Poisoning (NSP)

**Causative Toxins:** Brevetoxins (PbTx-1 through PbTx-10)
**Primary Producer:** Karenia brevis (dinoflagellate)
**Mode of Action:** Binds to voltage-gated sodium channels (site 5), causing persistent activation
**Symptoms:** Nausea, paresthesia, reversal of hot/cold sensation, ataxia, bradycardia
**Onset:** 30 minutes to 3 hours after ingestion
**Duration:** Usually 24-72 hours; no fatalities reported
**Regulatory Limit:** 800 μg/kg shellfish meat (20 Mouse Units/100g)
**Detection Methods:** LC-MS/MS, ELISA, receptor binding assay, mouse bioassay (deprecated)
**Stability:** Heat-stable, acid-stable

## Paralytic Shellfish Poisoning (PSP)

**Causative Toxins:** Saxitoxins (STX, neoSTX, GTX1-4, dcSTX, C-toxins) — over 57 congeners
**Primary Producers:** Alexandrium spp., Pyrodinium bahamense, Gymnodinium catenatum
**Mode of Action:** Blocks voltage-gated sodium channels (site 1), preventing action potentials
**Symptoms:** Perioral tingling, numbness, ataxia, respiratory paralysis in severe cases
**Onset:** 30 minutes to 2 hours
**Duration:** Days to weeks; mortality 1-10% in severe cases
**Regulatory Limit:** 800 μg STX eq/kg shellfish meat
**Detection Methods:** LC-FLD (pre/post-column oxidation), LC-MS/MS, receptor binding assay
**Stability:** Heat-stable, water-soluble, persists for months

## Diarrhetic Shellfish Poisoning (DSP)

**Causative Toxins:** Okadaic acid (OA), dinophysistoxins (DTX-1, DTX-2, DTX-3)
**Primary Producers:** Dinophysis spp., Prorocentrum spp.
**Mode of Action:** Inhibits protein phosphatases PP1 and PP2A, tumor promoter
**Symptoms:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
**Onset:** 30 minutes to 12 hours
**Duration:** 24-72 hours; rarely fatal
**Regulatory Limit:** 160 μg OA eq/kg shellfish meat
**Detection Methods:** LC-MS/MS, ELISA, phosphatase inhibition assay, mouse bioassay (deprecated)
**Stability:** Heat-stable, lipophilic; DTX-3 esters require hydrolysis for detection

## Amnesic Shellfish Poisoning (ASP)

**Causative Toxins:** Domoic acid (DA), isodomoic acids
**Primary Producers:** Pseudo-nitzschia spp. (diatoms)
**Mode of Action:** Glutamate receptor agonist (kainate/AMPA subtypes), neuroexcitotoxic
**Symptoms:** Gastrointestinal distress, confusion, memory loss (short-term), seizures, coma
**Onset:** 30 minutes to 24 hours after ingestion (neurological: 24-48 hours)
**Duration:** Neurological effects may be permanent; mortality ~2%
**Regulatory Limit:** 20 mg/kg shellfish meat
**Detection Methods:** LC-UV, LC-MS/MS, ELISA
**Stability:** Heat-stable, water-soluble

## Ciguatera Fish Poisoning (CFP)

**Causative Toxins:** Ciguatoxins (CTX-1 through CTX-4), maitotoxins
**Primary Producers:** Gambierdiscus spp., Fukuyoa spp. (benthic dinoflagellates)
**Mode of Action:** Binds to voltage-gated sodium channels (site 5), persistent activation
**Symptoms:** Gastrointestinal, neurological, cardiovascular; reversal of temperature sensation
**Onset:** 1-8 hours (GI), 24-48 hours (neurological)
**Duration:** Weeks to years; chronic symptoms possible
**Regulatory Limit:** 0.01 μg CTX-1B eq/kg fish (EU); 0.1 μg/kg (FDA action level)
**Detection Methods:** LC-MS/MS, N2a neuroblastoma assay, receptor binding assay
**Stability:** Heat-stable, lipophilic, bioaccumulates through food web

## Azaspiracid Shellfish Poisoning (AZP)

**Causative Toxins:** Azaspiracids (AZA-1 through AZA-40+)
**Primary Producers:** Azadinium spp., Amphidoma spp.
**Mode of Action:** Multiple organ toxicity; inhibits hERG potassium channels
**Symptoms:** Nausea, vomiting, severe diarrhea, stomach cramps
**Onset:** 2-24 hours
**Duration:** 2-5 days; no fatalities reported
**Regulatory Limit:** 160 μg AZA eq/kg shellfish meat (EU)
**Detection Methods:** LC-MS/MS (required — no screening methods available)
**Stability:** Heat-stable, lipophilic

## Hepatotoxins (Microcystins & Nodularins)

**Causative Toxins:** Microcystins (MC-LR, MC-RR, MC-YR, >250 congeners), Nodularin
**Primary Producers:** Microcystis, Planktothrix, Anabaena/Dolichospermum, Nodularia (cyanobacteria)
**Mode of Action:** Inhibits protein phosphatases PP1 and PP2A; hepatotoxic, tumor promoter
**Symptoms:** Liver damage, gastroenteritis, potential chronic effects
**Regulatory Limit:** 1.0 μg/L MC-LR in drinking water (WHO); shellfish limits under development
**Detection Methods:** LC-MS/MS, ELISA, PPIA (protein phosphatase inhibition assay)
**Stability:** Highly stable, resistant to boiling and chemical hydrolysis
