# Harmful Algal Bloom (HAB) Species Reference

## Overview

This reference document provides comprehensive information about harmful algal bloom species relevant to aquaculture monitoring and safety assessment. This document is used by the sub-skills for accurate species identification, toxin profiling, and risk assessment.

---

## Major HAB Species Groups

### Dinoflagellates

#### Karenia brevis
- **Common Name**: Florida red tide
- **Toxins**: Brevetoxins (PbTx)
- **Health Effects**: Neurotoxic Shellfish Poisoning (NSP)
- **Detection Threshold**: >5,000 cells/L
- **Bloom Season**: Late summer - fall (Gulf of Mexico)
- **Habitat**: Coastal, oligotrophic to mesotrophic waters
- **Temperature Range**: 20-30°C
- **Salinity Range**: 30-35 PSU
- **Satellite Signatures**: High chlorophyll anomaly, backscattering

#### Alexandrium spp. (A. fundyense, A. catenella, A. minutum)
- **Common Name**: Paralytic shellfish poison (PSP) dinoflagellates
- **Toxins**: Saxitoxins (STX) - gonyautoxins, neosaxitoxin
- **Health Effects**: Paralytic Shellfish Poisoning (PSP)
- **Detection Threshold**: >1,000 cells/L
- **Bloom Season**: Spring - summer (temperate), year-round (tropics)
- **Habitat**: Coastal, estuarine, stratified waters
- **Temperature Range**: 10-25°C
- **Salinity Range**: 20-32 PSU
- **Cyst Formation**: Yes (overwintering strategy)

#### Dinophysis spp. (D. acuminata, D. fortii, D. acuta)
- **Common Name**: Diarrhetic shellfish poison (DSP) producers
- **Toxins**: Okadaic acid (OA), dinophysistoxins (DTX)
- **Health Effects**: Diarrhetic Shellfish Poisoning (DSP)
- **Detection Threshold**: >200 cells/L
- **Bloom Season**: Late spring - fall
- **Habitat**: Coastal, upwelling areas
- **Temperature Range**: 15-25°C
- **Salinity Range**: 28-35 PSU
- **Mixotrophic**: Yes (both photosynthesis and feeding)

### Diatoms

#### Pseudo-nitzschia spp. (P. australis, P. multiseries, P. delicatissima)
- **Common Name**: Amnesic shellfish poison (ASP) producers
- **Toxins**: Domoic acid (DA)
- **Health Effects**: Amnesic Shellfish Poisoning (ASP)
- **Detection Threshold**: >10,000 cells/L
- **Bloom Season**: Spring - summer (upwelling regions)
- **Habitat**: Coastal, upwelling, nutrient-rich waters
- **Temperature Range**: 12-22°C
- **Salinity Range**: 25-33 PSU
- **Silica Requirement**: High (diatom frustules)

### Cyanobacteria (Blue-green algae)

#### Microcystis aeruginosa
- **Common Name**: Microcystin producer
- **Toxins**: Microcystins (MC-LR, etc.)
- **Health Effects**: Hepatotoxicity, liver damage
- **Detection Threshold**: >20,000 cells/mL (freshwater)
- **Bloom Season**: Summer (warm, stratified)
- **Habitat**: Freshwater, brackish, eutrophic waters
- **Temperature Range**: 20-30°C
- **Nitrogen Fixation**: No (requires nitrogen sources)

## Toxin Profiles

### Brevetoxins (PbTx)
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclic polyether
- **Molecular Weight**: ~900 Da
- **Solubility**: Lipophilic
- **Stability**: Moderate (degrades in weeks)
- **Bioaccumulation**: Yes (in shellfish)
- **Cooking Resistance**: Stable to cooking
- **Detection Methods**: ELISA, LC-MS, mouse bioassay (historical)
- **Regulatory Limit**: 800 μg/kg (shellfish meat)

### Saxitoxins (STX)
- **Chemical Class**: Tetrahydropurine
- **Molecular Weight**: ~300 Da
- **Solubility**: Water-soluble
- **Stability**: High (persists for months)
- **Bioaccumulation**: Yes (especially in viscera)
- **Cooking Resistance**: Stable to cooking
- **Detection Methods**: MBA (mouse bioassay), LC-MS, receptor binding assay
- **Regulatory Limit**: 800 μg STX eq/kg (shellfish meat)

### Okadaic Acid (OA)
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclic fatty acid
- **Molecular Weight**: ~800 Da
- **Solubility**: Lipophilic
- **Stability**: High (persists for months)
- **Bioaccumulation**: Yes (in digestive gland)
- **Cooking Resistance**: Stable to cooking
- **Detection Methods**: LC-MS, ELISA, MBA
- **Regulatory Limit**: 160 μg OA eq/kg (shellfish meat)

### Domoic Acid (DA)
- **Chemical Class**: Amino acid
- **Molecular Weight**: ~411 Da
- **Solubility**: Water-soluble
- **Stability**: High (persists for months)
- **Bioaccumulation**: Yes (especially in viscera)
- **Cooking Resistance**: Stable to cooking
- **Detection Methods**: LC-MS, ELISA
- **Regulatory Limit**: 20 mg/kg (shellfish meat)

### Microcystins (MC)
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclic heptapeptide
- **Molecular Weight**: ~1000 Da
- **Solubility**: Variable (depending on variant)
- **Stability**: High (resistant to degradation)
- **Bioaccumulation**: Limited (mainly in liver)
- **Cooking Resistance**: Generally stable
- **Detection Methods**: ELISA, LC-MS, PPIA
- **Regulatory Limit**: 1 μg/L (drinking water)

## Environmental Triggers

### Temperature
- **Optimal Range**: 18-28°C for most species
- **Stratification**: Warm surface layer promotes buoyant species
- **Seasonal Patterns**: Spring warming → diatoms; Summer warming → dinoflagellates
- **Climate Change**: Warming oceans expand HAB ranges poleward

### Nutrients
- **Nitrogen (N)**: Essential for growth; nitrate preferred by diatoms, ammonium by dinoflagellates
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Often limiting; N:P ratio matters
- **Silica (Si)**: Required for diatoms (frustules)
- **Iron (Fe)**: Limiting in some oceanic regions
- **Sources**: Runoff, upwelling, atmospheric deposition

### Salinity
- **Optimal Range**: 20-35 PSU for coastal species
- **Stratification**: Low-salinity lens retains buoyant cells
- **Estuarine**: Gradients create niche opportunities
- **Events**: Freshwater input (rain, river discharge) affects blooms

### Hydrodynamics
- **Upwelling**: Brings nutrients → diatom blooms
- **Stratification**: Retains cells near surface
- **Fronts**: Aggregate cells, promote growth
- **Currents**: Transport blooms to new areas
- **Residence Time**: Longer retention → bloom development

### Light
- **Optimal Range**: 100-300 μE/m²/s
- **Photoperiod**: Longer days promote growth
- **Mixing Depth**: Affects light availability
- **Self-shading**: High biomass limits light penetration

## Detection Methods

### Satellite Remote Sensing

#### Chlorophyll-a (Chl-a)
- **Sensor**: MODIS, VIIRS, Sentinel-3 OLCI, MERIS (historical)
- **Algorithm**: OC3, OC4 (ocean color)
- **Threshold**: >10 mg/m³ indicates potential bloom
- **Limitations**: CDOM interference, bottom reflectance in shallow water

#### Fluorescence Line Height (FLH)
- **Sensor**: MODIS, VIIRS
- **Advantage**: Less affected by CDOM
- **Specificity**: High for phytoplankton

#### Red Tide Indices
- **Algorithm**: Backscattering, absorption ratios
- **Example**: K. brevis red tide index (Florida)
- **Specificity**: Variable by species

### In Situ Methods

#### Fluorometry
- **Parameter**: Chlorophyll fluorescence
- **Advantage**: Real-time, high-frequency
- **Calibration**: Required against extracted chl-a
- **Deployment**: Moorings, profilers, AUVs

#### Flow Cytometry
- **Application**: Cell counting, size distribution
- **Advantage**: Species identification with pigments
- **Limitation**: Requires sample collection

#### qPCR
- **Target**: Species-specific DNA sequences
- **Advantage**: High sensitivity, specific
- **Turnaround**: Hours to days
- **Application**: Early warning, cyst mapping

#### Microscopy
- **Method**: Light, electron, immunofluorescence
- **Advantage**: Definitive identification
- **Limitation**: Time-consuming, requires expertise
- **Automation**: Imaging flow cytometry (e.g., Imaging FlowCytobot)

### Toxin Detection

#### Chemical Methods
- **LC-MS**: Gold standard, multi-toxin
- **HPLC-UV**: Historical, less sensitive
- **ELISA**: Rapid, field-deployable
- **Receptor Binding**: Functional assay

#### Biological Methods
- **Mouse Bioassay (MBA)**: Historical, ethical concerns
- **Cell Bioassay**: Cytotoxicity
- **Neuroblastoma**: Specific for brevetoxins

## Aquaculture Impact Assessment

### Species-Specific Vulnerability

#### Shellfish
- **Oysters**: Accumulate toxins in viscera; moderate depuration
- **Mussels**: Rapid accumulation, variable depuration
- **Clams**: Variable (species-specific)
- **Scallops**: Adductor muscle safe, viscera toxic

#### Fish
- **Direct Mortality**: Gill damage, oxygen depletion
- **Behavior**: Avoidance, reduced feeding
- **Toxin Transfer**: Through food web

### Economic Impact
- **Harvest Closures**: Days to months
- **Market Confidence**: Long-term effects
- **Monitoring Costs**: Routine testing required
- **Depuration**: Time and infrastructure

### Management Strategies
- **Monitoring**: Regular cell counts and toxin testing
- **Early Warning**: Satellite + in situ sensors
- **Selective Harvest**: Time area closures
- **Depuration**: Controlled conditions (species-specific)
- **Relocation**: Move stock to clean areas

## Geographic Distribution

### Global Patterns
- **Temperate**: Seasonal blooms (spring/summer)
- **Tropical**: Year-round potential
- **Upwelling Systems**: High productivity, frequent blooms
- **Enclosed Seas**: Longer residence times

### Regional Hotspots
- **Gulf of Mexico**: Karenia brevis (annual)
- **New England**: Alexandrium fundyense (seasonal)
- **West Coast USA**: Pseudo-nitzschia (upwelling)
- **Europe**: Dinophysis (Atlantic)
- **Asia**: Multiple species (diverse coastlines)
- **New Zealand**: Karenia (summer)
- **Chile**: Alexandrium, Pseudo-nitzschia

### Emerging Regions
- **Arctic**: Warming enables new species
- **Previously Unaffected**: Range expansions documented
- **Aquaculture Expansion**: New areas at risk

## Response Protocols

### Alert Levels

#### Level 1: Background (0-1,000 cells/L)
- **Action**: Routine monitoring
- **Frequency**: Weekly
- **Testing**: Visual inspection, periodic cell counts

#### Level 2: Elevated (1,000-5,000 cells/L)
- **Action**: Enhanced monitoring
- **Frequency**: 2-3 times per week
- **Testing**: Cell counts, toxin screening

#### Level 3: Bloom (5,000-50,000 cells/L)
- **Action**: Pre-emptive harvest consideration
- **Frequency**: Daily monitoring
- **Testing**: Cell counts, toxin quantification
- **Notification**: Industry stakeholders

#### Level 4: Major Bloom (>50,000 cells/L)
- **Action**: Harvest closure
- **Frequency**: Continuous monitoring
- **Testing**: Toxin quantification required
- **Public Health**: Advisory issued

### Mitigation Measures
- **Harvest Timing**: Early harvest before bloom peak
- **Site Selection**: Deeper water, offshore
- **Water Exchange**: Flushing, circulation
- **Clay Flocculation**: Sink cells (species-specific)
- **Biological Control**: Limited (research stage)

## Reference Thresholds

### Cell Count Thresholds (cells/L)
- **Karenia brevis**: 5,000 (bloom), 100,000 (major)
- **Alexandrium spp.**: 1,000 (bloom), 10,000 (major)
- **Dinophysis spp.**: 200 (bloom), 1,000 (major)
- **Pseudo-nitzschia**: 10,000 (bloom), 100,000 (major)

### Toxin Regulatory Limits
- **Brevetoxins**: 800 μg/kg (NSP)
- **Saxitoxins**: 800 μg STX eq/kg (PSP)
- **Okadaic Acid**: 160 μg OA eq/kg (DSP)
- **Domoic Acid**: 20 mg/kg (ASP)

### Satellite Chl-a Thresholds (mg/m³)
- **Background**: <5
- **Elevated**: 5-10
- **Bloom**: >10
- **Major Bloom**: >50 (localized)

## Seasonal Patterns by Region

### Gulf of Mexico
- **Spring**: Diatoms, moderate HAB activity
- **Summer**: Karenia brevis bloom onset
- **Fall**: K. brevis bloom peak, potential transport
- **Winter**: Low activity, cyst settlement

### New England
- **Spring**: Alexandrium germination, bloom onset
- **Summer**: Mixed community, lower toxicity
- **Fall**: Potential fall bloom, senescence
- **Winter**: Cyst dormancy, low activity

### West Coast USA
- **Spring**: Upwelling, Pseudo-nitzschia blooms
- **Summer**: Continued upwelling, mixed blooms
- **Fall**: Relaxation, potential transport
- **Winter**: Low activity, deep mixing

### Global Patterns
- **Temperate**: Seasonal stratification drives blooms
- **Tropical**: Less seasonal, rainfall-driven
- **Upwelling**: Wind-driven, seasonal productivity
- **High Latitude**: Short season, expanding with warming
